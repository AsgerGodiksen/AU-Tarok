================================================================
  HOW TO READ THESE NUMBERS
================================================================

  STEADY (Nm)  — The average torque the motor holds at the end of the
    interval. This is the 'resting load' once things have calmed down.
    A value close to zero means the joint is nearly unloaded.
    A large value means the motor is working hard just to hold position.

  PK-PK (Nm)  — The total swing from the lowest to the highest torque
    value seen. Think of it as 'how wild was the signal?'
    Small Pk-Pk = the joint is calm and stable.
    Large Pk-Pk = the joint is bouncing around a lot.
    Goal: get this as small as possible without slowing the response.

  RMSE (Nm)  — On average, how far was the torque from its steady value?
    Lower is better. This is your main PID performance score —
    if two tests have the same Pk-Pk, the one with lower RMSE
    spent less time far from the target.

  STD (Nm)  — How much the torque 'jitters' around its average.
    A low Std but high RMSE means the signal is smooth but offset.
    A high Std means the signal is noisy or still oscillating.
    Compare Std between tests with the same PID gains to judge noise.

  FREQFFT (Hz)  — The dominant oscillation frequency found by
    mathematically decomposing the whole signal (FFT method).
    FreqPk finds the same thing by measuring the time between peaks.
    If both agree -> the signal has a clear, repeating oscillation.
    If they disagree -> the oscillation is irregular or fading out.
    Higher frequency = faster wobbling. A PID that is too aggressive
    (high P or I) will push this frequency up.

  SETTLE (s)  — How many seconds passed before the torque stayed
    within +-0.3 Nm of its final value and did not leave again.
    This is how long the robot took to 'settle in' to its pose.
    Shorter = better. A PID that never settles will show 'N/A'.
    Tip: if Settle is long but Pk-Pk is small, the robot is slow but
    stable — try increasing P a little.

  OVERSHOOT (%)  — How far past the final resting torque did the signal
    fly before settling? Expressed as a percentage of the steady value.
    +120 % means the torque peaked at more than double its final value.
    Negative sign means it undershot (went the wrong way first).
    High overshoot = too much P or too little D.
    Near zero overshoot with long Settle = too much D (overdamped).

  QUICK TUNING GUIDE
  ------------------
  Oscillating / high Pk-Pk and Std  -> reduce P or increase D
  Slow to settle / low overshoot    -> increase P or reduce D
  Steady-state offset (Steady != 0) -> increase I
  Noisy but stable                  -> reduce P slightly, check D filter
  FreqFFT and FreqPk very different -> oscillation is irregular,
                                       focus on Settle and RMSE instead
================================================================
